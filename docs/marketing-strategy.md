## Section 3 — Marketing Channel Strategy

### 3.1 — LinkedIn (Highest Priority)
This is where LOs and mortgage professionals spend time. Start here.

**Content types that work:**
- Before/after stories: "What my Monday morning used to look like vs. now"
- Screenshot walkthroughs: "Here's how I generate a pre-approval email in 30 seconds"
- The origin story: "I replaced my $300/mo CRM and my admin with this"
- Industry pain points: "Why most LO tech stacks are broken"
- Transparency posts: "What LoanOS looked like 6 months ago vs. today"

**Cadence:** 3–4 posts/week minimum. Document the build process publicly — people follow the journey.

**Who to connect with:** Mortgage brokers, independent LOs, branch managers, mortgage coaches, real estate agents who have LO relationships.

### 3.2 — Facebook Groups
Several high-activity Facebook groups exist for independent mortgage brokers:
- "Brokers Are Better" (UWM broker network)
- "The Mortgage Calculator" community
- State-level mortgage broker associations

Strategy: Don't pitch immediately. Contribute value first. Answer questions. When someone complains about Jungo or manual follow-up, that's your opening.

### 3.3 — YouTube / Short-Form Video
LOs search for software reviews and workflow tutorials on YouTube.

**High-value video ideas:**
- "I replaced Jungo with this" (title that gets searched)
- "LoanOS vs. Mortgage Coach — what's actually different"
- "Watch me draft a pre-approval email with AI (30 seconds)"
- "My full LO tech stack for 2026"
- "How I automated my mortgage follow-up"

Short-form (Reels/TikTok/YouTube Shorts) of specific workflows — each under 60 seconds, each showing one automation doing its thing.

### 3.4 — Email Marketing (Mailchimp)
You already have Mailchimp set up. Build two lists:
- **LO prospect list** — mortgage professionals who haven't signed up yet
- **Early access waitlist** — people who want in when Phase 4 launches

**Email sequences to build:**

*Welcome sequence (5 emails):*
1. Welcome + the origin story (why LoanOS exists)
2. The $400/month problem (software stack cost breakdown)
3. Feature spotlight: AI email automation
4. Feature spotlight: Daily Briefing
5. "We're opening spots in Phase 4 — want one?"

*Nurture sequence (ongoing):*
- Monthly product update email (what got built this month)
- Case study emails (Adam's own production results)
- Tips on mortgage automation, LO efficiency

### 3.5 — Mortgage Industry Podcasts
Several podcasts target independent LOs and mortgage brokers. Getting Adam on as a guest (talking about AI + mortgage, not selling LoanOS directly) builds credibility and audience.

**Podcast targets:**
- The Mortgage Calculator Podcast
- Mortgage Marketing Radio
- Broker to Broker
- LO Intelligence Podcast
- The Loan Officer Podcast

Pitch angle: "How I replaced my admin with AI automation and what that actually looks like"

### 3.6 — Referrals from Real Estate Agents
Adam already has relationships with 100+ realtors in Austin. These are warm introductions.

**Idea:** When an agent sees LoanOS in action (automated referral intro emails, milestone notifications), they have an incentive to refer their other LO contacts. Consider a referral incentive for agents who connect you with a new LO subscriber.

**Above the fold:**
- Headline: "The mortgage platform built by the LO who uses it every day"
- Subhead: "Replace your CRM, your presentation software, and your manual follow-up — with one AI-powered tool"

Give away something valuable to build the email list before launch.

- Covers: how to evaluate your current stack, what features to demand from a CRM, what automation is worth building vs. buying

"10 mortgage email templates that get responses" — pre-approval emails, realtor updates, follow-ups, post-closing review requests. The catch: LoanOS writes all of these automatically.

**Option C: Video Mini-Course**
"Automate your mortgage follow-up in 5 days" — a free 5-day email course with one video per day showing a specific automation workflow. Each video ends with "and here's what this looks like inside LoanOS."

**Recommended:** Start with Option A (easiest to produce) and build Option C once LoanOS is in Phase 4 (most powerful for conversion).

## Section 7 — Sales Outreach Approach

Once you have the waitlist and a Phase 4 launch date, this is the outreach playbook.

**Warm outreach (existing network):**
1. Personal LinkedIn DM to LOs Adam already knows: "Hey — I built something I think you'd actually use. Would you be open to a 20-minute demo?"
2. Personal email to the same list
3. Follow-up with anyone who engaged with LinkedIn content

**Cold outreach (new LOs):**
Target LOs who have posted complaints about their current software on LinkedIn, Facebook groups, or industry forums. The opening line writes itself: "Saw your post about [Jungo/manual follow-up/software costs] — that's literally why I built this."

**Demo close:**
End every demo with a specific ask: "We're opening Phase 4 to a small group first. I'd rather give you a founding member spot than have you wait. What would you need to see to move forward?"

## Section 8 — Pricing Ideas (for Phase 4)

You haven't priced this yet — here's a framework to start thinking about it.

**Comparable tools and their pricing:**
- Jungo: ~$150–300/month
- BNTouch: $79–149/month
- Mortgage Coach: $99/month
- Zapier (for automation): $50–100/month

**Combined:** LOs who have all three are paying $300–550/month.

**LoanOS positioning options:**

| Tier | Price | Target | What's Included |
|------|-------|--------|----------------|
| Solo | $99/mo | Independent LO, <30 loans/year | Full platform, 1 user |
| Pro | $149/mo | Active LO, 30–60 loans/year | Full platform + AI agent credits |
| Team | $299/mo | Small team, 2–5 LOs | Multi-user, white-label emails, team reporting |

**Founding member pricing:** Lock early adopters in at Solo ($79/mo) or Pro ($99/mo) for life if they join before official launch. Creates urgency and rewards early believers.

## Section 9 — Metrics to Track

Before you can improve marketing, you need to know what's working.

**Awareness metrics:**
- LinkedIn follower growth, post impressions, engagement rate
- YouTube views, watch time, subscriber count
- Email list size, open rate, click rate

**Pipeline metrics:**
- Waitlist signups per month
- Demo requests
- Demo-to-signup conversion rate

**Revenue metrics (Phase 4):**
- MRR (monthly recurring revenue)
- Churn rate
- Net Promoter Score (LO satisfaction)

**Benchmark:** At $149/month average, 100 paying LOs = $14,900 MRR / $178,800 ARR. That's a meaningful business on top of Adam's production income.

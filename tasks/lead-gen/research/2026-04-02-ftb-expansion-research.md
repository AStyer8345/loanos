# Research: First-Time Buyer DPA Expansion — Lead Generation
Date: 2026-04-02
Topic: Texas DPA Programs + FTB Funnel Design for Austin Market

---

## Executive Summary

Austin FTBs in 2026 have access to a stacked set of DPA programs that can cover $20,000–$40,000+ in down payment and closing costs — but most buyers don't know these programs exist or believe they won't qualify. The median Austin household earns ~$93,000 and can technically afford a ~$300K home, but the median sale price is ~$412,000–$430,000, creating a real gap that DPA directly bridges. The City of Austin's program (up to $40,000 forgivable) and Travis County's Hill Country Home DPA (4–6% forgivable) are the strongest local options and are currently OPEN and funded as of April 2026. The biggest funnel opportunity is myth-busting: the "I need 20% down" myth alone disqualifies thousands of ready buyers. Lead with "How Austin Buyers Are Getting Into Their First Home With $0–5K Out of Pocket" — that's the hook that cuts through.

## Texas DPA Programs Available in 2026

### 1. TDHCA My First Texas Home
- **Who runs it:** Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs (TDHCA)
- **Assistance amount:** Up to 5% of the loan amount as a 30-year, 0% interest second mortgage (deferred; forgiven if borrower stays in home)
- **Loan types:** FHA, VA, USDA, or conventional (Fannie/Freddie)
- **Income limits:** Vary by county and household size. For Travis County: limits are set at a percentage of AMFI. The TDHCA Limits PDF (updated Feb 2026) is the authoritative source. Generally, must be below ~80–115% AMFI depending on area designation.
- **Credit score minimum:** 620 (most participating lenders require 640 in practice)
- **First-time buyer requirement:** Yes — must not have owned a home in the past 3 years (waived for veterans)
- **Purchase price limits:** Set by TDHCA and vary by county; Travis County limits are moderate (see TSAHC comparison below)
- **Funding status:** OPEN and active as of April 2026 — first-come, first-served, subject to funding availability
- **Can combine with MCC?** YES — the MCC can be layered on top of My First Texas Home
- **Source:** [TDHCA My First Texas Home Program Matrix (Feb 2026)](https://welcomehome.tdhca.texas.gov/uploads/My-First-Texas-Home-Program-Matrix-TMS.pdf?v=1738018341)

### 2. TDHCA Texas Mortgage Credit Certificate (MCC)
- **What it is:** A federal tax credit equal to 15% of mortgage interest paid annually — for the life of the loan. Dollar-for-dollar reduction in federal income tax liability each year.
- **Example:** On a $300,000 loan at 6.5% interest, first-year interest ~$19,500. 15% MCC = ~$2,925 direct tax credit.
- **Who qualifies:** First-time buyers (same 3-year rule), must meet income and purchase price limits by county
- **Can combine with DPA?** YES — MCC can be stacked with My First Texas Home DPA (2nd lien). This is one of the most powerful combinations available in Texas.
- **Applies through:** Participating lenders; borrower claims via IRS Form 8396 at tax time
- **Funding status:** OPEN — applied for at time of mortgage application
- **Source:** [TDHCA MCC Program Page](https://welcomehome.tdhca.texas.gov/programs/texas-mortgage-credit-certificate-program)

### 3. City of Austin Homebuyer Assistance Program (DPA)
- **Assistance amount:** Up to **$40,000** — the largest locally available grant/forgivable loan in the Austin market
- **Structure:** 0% interest, deferred forgivable second lien
  - Loans up to $14,900: forgiven after 5 years
  - Loans up to $40,000: forgiven after 10 years
- **Income limits (approximate 2025/2026):**
  - 1-person: $54,700
  - 2-person: $62,500
  - 3-person: $70,300
  - 4-person: $78,100
  - 5-person: $84,350
  - (Based on ~80% of Austin AMI — verify current figures at austintexas.gov)
- **Geographic restriction:** Must be within Austin city limits
- **First-time buyer requirement:** Yes (no home ownership in last 3 years)
- **Funding status:** OPEN as of April 2026. Contact City of Austin Housing Dept: 512-974-3100
- **Note:** Income limits are conservative (80% AMI). Many Austin buyers earning $80K–$93K will not qualify. This program serves lower-income FTBs specifically.
- **Source:** [AustinTexas.gov Homebuyer Resources](https://www.austintexas.gov/homebuyer)

### 4. Travis County Hill Country Home DPA
- **Who runs it:** Travis County Housing Finance Corporation
- **Assistance amount:** 4%, 5%, or 6% of the initial principal balance
- **Structure:** 0% interest, 10-year forgivable second mortgage
- **Eligible area:** All of Travis County (including City of Austin, Bee Cave, Lakeway, etc.)
- **Loan types:** FHA, VA, USDA-RD, or Freddie Mac HFA Advantage
- **Income limit:** Max qualifying income ≤ 140% AMI = ~$138,460 (current)
- **Credit score minimum:** 640 FICO
- **DTI limit:** 45% maximum
- **Purchase price limit:** $364,452 — this is a key constraint for Austin buyers given current median prices
- **First-time buyer requirement:** Yes; all FTBs must complete HUD-approved homebuyer education
- **Funding status:** OPEN as of April 2026
- **Source:** [Travis County Corporations Homebuyers Page](https://corporations.traviscountytx.gov/homebuyers)

### 5. TSAHC Home Sweet Texas Home Loan Program
- **Who runs it:** Texas State Affordable Housing Corporation (TSAHC) — separate from TDHCA
- **Assistance amount:** Up to 5% of loan amount; can choose between:
  - **Grant** (true gift — no repayment ever, no lien)
  - **Deferred forgivable second lien** (forgiven after 3 years if borrower stays)
- **Income limits for Travis County (2025, effective May 2025):**
  - Non-targeted areas: $167,250 (any family size)
  - Targeted areas: $187,320 (any family size)
  - Note: Much higher limits than City of Austin program — reaches middle-income buyers
- **Purchase price limits for Travis County:**
  - Non-targeted: $593,364
  - Targeted: $725,222
- **Credit score minimum:** 620
- **First-time buyer requirement:** NOT required for Home Sweet Texas (also available to repeat buyers)
- **Funding status:** OPEN and active
- **Can combine with MCC?** YES — TSAHC also offers its own MCC program
- **Source:** [TSAHC Home Buyer Programs](https://www.tsahc.org/home-buyer-programs) | [Income/Purchase Price Limits PDF](https://www.tsahc.org/public/upload/files/general/TSAHC_Combined_Income_Purchase_Price_Limits.pdf)

### 6. SETH (Southeast Texas Housing Finance Corporation) Programs
- **GoldStar / 5 Star Texas Advantage:** SETH offers up to 7% DPA via forgivable second mortgage (3, 7, or 10-year terms)
- **Travis County / Austin eligibility:** **SETH GoldStar is NOT available in Travis County or the City of Austin.** Properties must be outside Travis County to use SETH programs.
- **Verdict for Adam's market:** Not applicable for most Austin-proper clients. Relevant for clients looking in surrounding counties (Williamson, Hays, Bastrop, etc.)
- **Source:** [SETHFC Programs](https://sethfc.com/programs/5-star-texas-advantage/)

### 7. Stacking / Combining Programs
- **DPA + MCC:** YES — TDHCA My First Texas Home + MCC can be combined. TSAHC programs + TSAHC MCC can be combined. This is the power play.
- **Multiple DPA grants stacked:** Generally NO — you cannot layer two down payment grants on the same loan
- **DPA + FHA:** YES — all major Texas DPA programs are FHA-compatible
- **DPA + Conventional:** YES — TSAHC and TDHCA both allow conventional (Fannie/Freddie) with DPA
- **City of Austin + State DPA:** Generally no — the City program functions as a second lien and lenders/programs limit the number of seconds
- **Maximum realistic stack (Austin):** TSAHC 5% grant (no repayment) + TSAHC MCC (15% annual tax credit) = strongest combination for buyers above City of Austin income limits

## Down Payment Myth Content Angles

### Myth 1: "I Need 20% Down to Buy a Home"
- **The myth:** Buyers believe they must save $80,000–$100,000 before buying in Austin
- **The truth:** NAR data shows 73% of first-time buyers put down 15% or less. The typical FTB puts down 6–9%. FHA requires just 3.5%. Conventional is 3–5%. With DPA, some Austin buyers close with $0–$5K out of pocket.
- **The pain point:** 35% of buyers think they need 16–20% down. 10% think they need more than 20%. These buyers are sitting on the sideline for years waiting for savings that aren't necessary.
- **Email/funnel copy angle:** "You don't need $80,000 to buy a house in Austin. Here's what you actually need." — leads into DPA program breakdown
- **Source:** [NAR Financing Misconceptions](https://www.nar.realtor/blogs/economists-outlook/tackling-home-financing-and-down-payment-misconceptions) | [Rocket Mortgage FTB Study](https://www.rocketmortgage.com/learn/first-time-home-buyers-and-down-payments)

### Myth 2: "My Income Is Too Low to Qualify for DPA"
- **The myth:** DPA is for people on government assistance — not regular working people
- **The truth:** TSAHC Home Sweet Texas allows up to $167,250 household income in Travis County. That covers the vast majority of Austin households. Travis County Hill Country DPA goes up to $138,460. The City of Austin program has tighter limits (~$78K for a family of 4), but TSAHC and TDHCA cast a much wider net.
- **The pain point:** Middle-income buyers ($70K–$130K HHI) assume they make too much — they're actually the target demographic for TSAHC
- **Email/funnel copy angle:** "Think you make too much to qualify for down payment help? Run these 3 numbers first." — then show the income limit table

### Myth 3: "DPA Creates a High Monthly Payment Because of the Second Mortgage"
- **The myth:** DPA is a second mortgage with another monthly payment on top of your first — making housing unaffordable
- **The truth:** All major Texas DPA programs are 0% interest, deferred, with no monthly payment due. Grants (TSAHC) require no repayment at all, ever. Forgivable loans require no payment and are forgiven after 3–10 years if you stay in the home.
- **The pain point:** This myth is particularly sticky because it sounds logical — "of course there's a catch." In reality, the only "cost" is a forgiveness period.
- **Email/funnel copy angle:** "DPA is not a second payment every month. Here's what it actually is (and isn't)." — explain forgivable loan vs. grant with a visual
- **Source:** [TSAHC Grant vs. Second Lien Comparison](https://www.tsahc.org/blog/post/should-you-choose-a-down-payment-grant-or-2nd-lien-that-is-the-question)

### Myth 4: "I Need Perfect Credit to Qualify"
- **The myth:** DPA programs are for buyers with 750+ credit scores
- **The truth:** TDHCA minimum is 620. TSAHC minimum is 620. Travis County Hill Country DPA minimum is 640. The City of Austin DPA doesn't publish a specific minimum (lender-determined). These are FTB-accessible thresholds, not luxury scores.
- **The pain point:** Many FTBs with 640–680 scores assume they can't buy. They can — especially with FHA + DPA.
- **Email/funnel copy angle:** "Your credit score might be enough right now. Here's the minimum for Austin's top DPA programs."

### Myth 5: "I Missed My Window — Austin Is Too Expensive for a First-Time Buyer"
- **The myth:** Austin's boom years permanently locked out FTBs; the ship has sailed
- **The truth:** Austin's median dropped from its 2022 peak ($550K+) to ~$412,000–$430,000 metro-wide. Inventory is rising. The market is more buyer-friendly than 2021–2022. With DPA stacking, a buyer earning $95K can realistically close on a $350,000–$400,000 home with minimal cash out of pocket.
- **The pain point:** Buyers who were burned by 2021–2022 are emotionally checked out
- **Email/funnel copy angle:** "Austin's market just changed. Here's what that means for first-time buyers in 2026."

## FTB Lead Magnet Best Practices

### Format: What Converts Best
- **Interactive calculators** outperform static PDFs by 200–300% on conversion, according to mortgage-specific lead gen data. "How much down payment do I actually need in Austin?" calculator > static guide PDF.
- **PDF guides** still convert well (avg ~18% opt-in rate on lead magnet landing pages), especially when the title solves a specific problem. Cheat-sheet format (one page, scannable) outperforms long-form guides.
- **Checklist format** converts particularly well for "what do I need to do before buying" angles. Lower perceived friction than a long guide.
- **Video is a trust builder**, not a primary lead capture format. Use it post-opt-in in Email 1 to increase stick rate, not as the initial lead magnet.
- **Recommendation for Adam's DPA funnel:** PDF guide (4–6 pages) with checklist inside. Title that names the specific dollar amount and Austin specifically. Offer both a "see if you qualify" CTA and the guide download.

### Headline Formulas That Work
- **Specificity formula:** "How Austin First-Time Buyers Are Getting $20,000–$40,000 in Down Payment Help (Most Have No Idea This Exists)"
- **Myth-bust formula:** "The '20% Down' Myth Is Costing Austin Buyers Years. Here's the Truth."
- **Number + location formula:** "5 Down Payment Programs Austin FTBs Can Stack in 2026"
- **Question format:** "Could You Buy an Austin Home With $5,000 Down? (More People Qualify Than You Think)"
- **Fear of missing out:** "Austin DPA Programs Are Open Right Now — But Not Forever"

### Opt-In Rate Benchmarks
- Strong DPA-themed landing pages: 15–25% opt-in rate (no paid traffic)
- With warm realtor referral traffic: 25–35% is achievable
- Best practice: Single field (email only) outperforms name + email on cold traffic. Add name field only after you have opt-in momentum.

### Landing Page Design Guidance
- Lead with the specific dollar amount in the headline ("up to $40,000" or "up to 5% of your purchase price")
- Include a short "do you qualify?" mini-checklist (3–4 bullets) on the page: First-time buyer? Credit 620+? Income under $X? Buying in Austin/Travis County? → Each checked box increases conversion
- Social proof: "I helped 47 Austin families use this program last year" (adjust to Adam's actual numbers)
- Single CTA above the fold
- Mobile-first design: FTB audiences are predominantly mobile users

## 8-Email Nurture Sequence Structure

Recommended cadence: 8 emails over 60 days. First 3 emails are fast (days 0–7) while the lead is warm. Then slow to weekly, then bi-weekly.

| # | Send Day | Type | Topic | Subject Line Approach |
|---|----------|------|-------|----------------------|
| 1 | Day 0 (immediate) | Deliver + Welcome | Guide delivery + quick wins | "Your Austin DPA guide is here (+ 1 thing to do today)" |
| 2 | Day 2 | Education | The "20% myth" debunked — what FTBs actually put down | "The myth that kept you renting (it's not what you think)" |
| 3 | Day 5 | Education | DPA programs explained simply — grants vs. forgivable loans | "Free money. No catch. Here's how Austin's DPA actually works." |
| 4 | Day 10 | Story/Trust | Real buyer story — someone Adam helped with DPA in Austin | "How [first name] bought their Austin home with $4,200 out of pocket" |
| 5 | Day 17 | Education + CTA | Credit score & income — "do I qualify?" breakdown | "Your credit score might already be DPA-eligible. Here's the threshold." |
| 6 | Day 25 | Market intel | Austin market update — why now vs. waiting | "What's changed in Austin's market (and why FTBs are moving)" |
| 7 | Day 38 | Soft CTA | The process — what getting pre-approved actually looks like | "It takes 15 minutes. Here's what happens when you get pre-approved." |
| 8 | Day 52 | Hard CTA | Direct ask — are you ready to see what you qualify for? | "Still renting? Let's run your numbers for free." |

**Notes on this structure:**
- Email 1: Deliver the lead magnet immediately. Include a "quick win" — one piece of actionable info they can use today. Sets the tone that you deliver value, not spam.
- Emails 2–3: Pure education. No CTA to apply. Build trust first.
- Email 4: Story/social proof is the conversion pivot email. Real buyer results beat facts every time.
- Email 5: The "qualification checklist" email. Readers self-select. Those who say "I think I qualify" move to Email 6 as warm leads.
- Emails 7–8: Transition to CTAs. Email 7 is soft ("here's what the process looks like"). Email 8 is the close.
- **Rule:** Every email has exactly ONE CTA. Don't stack CTAs. Don't ask them to call, reply, and schedule at the same time.
- **Hard CTA introduction:** Email 7 (Day 38). Not before. Building to it cold kills opt-in momentum.

## Austin FTB Buyer Profile

### Income
- Austin median household income: ~$93,658 (2025 data, Neilsberg)
- Travis County median HHI: ~$97,169
- This is 27% above state and 24% above US median — Austin buyers earn well, but home prices outpace incomes
- Source: [Neilsberg Austin Median Household Income](https://www.neilsberg.com/insights/austin-tx-median-household-income/)

### Home Prices and the Down Payment Gap
- Austin metro median sale price: ~$412,000–$430,000 (early 2026)
- City of Austin proper median: ~$522,500 (higher due to urban core inventory mix)
- At $412K, a 5% down payment = $20,600; closing costs add $8,000–$16,000 → total cash needed ~$29,000–$39,000
- Typical Austin FTB down payment (2024): ~$45,547 (NAR/Austin MLS data)
- The gap: Median Austin household can qualify for a ~$300K mortgage on pure debt-to-income, but median home costs $412K+ — DPA closes that gap on both the down payment AND qualifying price point
- Source: [Neuhaus Realty Austin Affordability 2026](https://neuhausre.com/income-needed-buy-home-austin-texas-2026/)

### Credit Score Range for DPA Applicants
- Minimum floor: 620 (TDHCA, TSAHC)
- Travis County Hill Country DPA: 640 minimum
- Most DPA applicants in Austin range 630–680 — squarely in the FHA sweet spot
- Above 680: Conventional + DPA is often better than FHA + DPA (no MIP)

### First-Time Buyer Market Share
- Nationally: FTBs dropped to a historic low of 21% of all buyers (NAR 2025 Profile, covering July 2024–June 2025)
- Average FTB age nationally: 40 years old (new record high, up from 33 in 2019)
- This low share = opportunity: fewer FTBs in the market means less competition from that segment AND they are underserved by most LOs who focus on move-up buyers
- Austin-specific FTB % not publicly available, but Austin was ranked the #1 large city in Texas for FTB friendliness (CultureMap Austin, 2025)
- Source: [NAR 2025 Profile — FTB Share Historic Low](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/first-time-home-buyer-share-falls-to-historic-low-of-21-median-age-rises-to-40)

## Recommended Approach

### Lead Magnet to Offer
**Title:** "The Austin First-Time Buyer DPA Guide: How to Get $20,000–$40,000 in Down Payment Help (And What Programs You Actually Qualify For)"

**Format:** 4–6 page PDF with a 1-page "Do I Qualify?" checklist at the back. Not a wall of text — scannable, bullet-heavy, specific dollar amounts.

**Hook that resonates:** Lead with the dollar amount and the surprise factor. Most Austin FTBs have never heard of these programs. The shock of "wait, $40,000 is just sitting here?" is the emotional trigger. Pair it with myth-busting ("and no, it's not a second payment every month").

### Funnel Architecture Recommendation
1. **Top of funnel:** Social posts/realtor referrals drive to a single-page landing page with "Download the Austin DPA Guide" opt-in
2. **Lead capture:** Email only (name optional) — minimize friction
3. **Immediate trigger:** Auto-deliver guide + Email 1 within 5 minutes (critical — first 10 minutes determine engagement)
4. **8-email nurture:** Over 60 days as outlined above
5. **Conversion event:** "Run your numbers" call/Calendly link introduced at Email 7
6. **Realtor referral angle:** Gift this guide to your top realtor partners as a co-branded client resource — double the distribution with zero additional work

### Which Program to Lead With
**TSAHC Home Sweet Texas** is the right hero program for Adam's funnel because:
- Income limits ($167,250 for Travis County) catch the widest audience
- Can be a grant (no repayment — cleanest marketing message)
- Purchase price limits ($593K+ in Travis County) cover most Austin home prices
- 620 minimum credit score
- Available to repeat buyers too (bonus audience)

Frame the City of Austin $40K program as the "if you're below this income level" option — powerful close for lower-income FTBs but not the top-of-funnel hero.

## Gap Analysis

### Gap 1: Purchase Price Ceiling Problem (HIGH IMPACT)
The Travis County Hill Country DPA caps at $364,452. With Austin median at $412,000+, this program misses the majority of current listings. The funnel needs to clearly distinguish which program fits which price range — otherwise buyers will feel misled when they find out the program doesn't cover their target home. Solution: "DPA program selector" logic in Email 3 or a landing page quiz.

### Gap 2: No Calculator or Interactive Tool (HIGH IMPACT)
Research shows interactive tools (calculators) convert 200–300% better than static PDFs for mortgage lead generation. A simple "How much DPA could you get?" calculator — taking purchase price and income as inputs — would dramatically outperform a PDF alone. This is the top upgrade to the funnel if engineering time allows.

### Gap 3: No Re-Engagement Path for "Stuck" Leads (MEDIUM IMPACT)
After Email 8 (Day 52), leads who haven't converted have no path back in. Industry best practice is to move them to a long-term "evergreen" sequence (monthly value email) rather than letting them go cold. Without this, the list depreciates rapidly over time. A monthly "Austin market update" email keeps Adam's name in front of leads for 6–12 months until they're ready to move.

## Open Questions for Adam

1. **Income limit fit:** What's the typical household income of your FTB leads? If most are $90K–$130K, TSAHC is the right hero program. If you're seeing more under $70K (perhaps realtor referrals from affordable neighborhoods), the City of Austin $40K program may deserve top billing.

2. **Purchase price range:** Are your FTB referrals typically targeting $300K–$365K homes (where Travis County Hill Country DPA fits) or $365K–$450K+ homes (where it doesn't)? This determines which DPA is most useful to highlight.

3. **Realtor co-brand:** Do you want to offer this DPA guide as a co-branded resource to realtor partners? If yes, we need a version with a blank logo slot or realtor name field — could be a strong referral partner value-add.

4. **Calculator vs. PDF:** Are you open to building a simple "DPA calculator" landing page (1–2 hours of dev time) instead of or in addition to the PDF? The conversion lift is significant and it's highly shareable on social.

5. **Mailchimp vs. n8n for the drip sequence:** The 8-email sequence can run through the existing LoanOS Drip Email Scheduler workflow in n8n, or through a dedicated Mailchimp automation. Which do you prefer — or should we build in n8n to keep it centralized in LoanOS?

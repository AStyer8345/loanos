## Sources Found

### 1. TDHCA Down Payment Assistance Programs
**URL:** https://www.tdhca.state.tx.us/homeownership/fthb/down-payment-assistance.htm
**Domain:** tdhca.state.tx.us (Texas Dept of Housing and Community Affairs — official)
**NotebookLM ID:** 9bd29445

**Summary:** Official TDHCA page listing state-sponsored DPA programs available to
first-time homebuyers in Texas. Includes My First Texas Home (down payment + closing cost
assistance), Texas Mortgage Credit Certificate (MCC — federal tax credit up to $2,000/year),
and My Choice Texas Home (not restricted to first-time buyers). Income and purchase price
limits vary by county. These programs are the #1 lead magnet opportunity for Austin FTB
targeting — a free guide to these programs is what makes the lead magnet genuinely valuable.

**Lead gen relevance:** HIGH — Austin first-time buyers consistently ask about DPA. A guide
specifically explaining TDHCA programs + how to qualify = compelling lead magnet.

### 3. HousingWire — What Will Bring First-Time Buyers Into 2026 Housing Market
**URL:** https://www.housingwire.com/articles/2026-first-time-homebuyers/
**Domain:** housingwire.com
**NotebookLM ID:** 04a9e2bb

**Summary:** HousingWire analysis of 2026 first-time buyer market dynamics. Key themes:
affordability remains the top barrier (rates sticky at 6.5-7%), millennial/Gen Z buyers
represent ~50% of purchase activity, and buyers increasingly want pre-approval before looking.
Lock-in effect continues to suppress move-up inventory, pushing more demand to entry-level.
Austin market: median home price ~$480K — tight for FTB with conventional (3-5% down).

**Lead gen relevance:** HIGH — Shapes funnel messaging. FTB in Austin at $480K needs DPA
education + creative financing (FHA 3.5%, conventional 3%, USDA if rural-adjacent). Lock-in
effect means realtor partners are hungry for FTB pre-approvals to fill their listings pipeline.
